In one sentence

HabitToll is an app blocker for iPhone and Android: before an app you picked opens, it asks “Worth it?” Walking away is free; 10 minutes cost a price you set, from €0.29.

In a paragraph

HabitToll is an app blocker for iPhone and Android, made by Comads OÜ in Tallinn, Estonia. People pick the apps they open too often, and from then on each one asks “Worth it?” before it opens. Walking away takes one tap and is counted. Going in costs 10 minutes at a price the person set, from €0.29, as a one-time in-app purchase: there is no subscription and there are no ads. A settings lock can make easier changes wait 24 hours or need a partner's code, and friends see what someone pays each day and their streak, never which apps.

How it works

  1. You pick the apps you open too often, and a price for 10 minutes, from €0.29.
  2. When you open one of them, HabitToll asks “Worth it?”
  3. Not worth it: one tap, free, and it counts as a walk-away. Worth it: 10 minutes at your price, then it asks again.
  4. The home screen shows how often you walked away this month, your streak and your monthly bill.

Getting the details right

  • An unlock is a purchase of time: 10 minutes, when an app is worth it to the person. Walking away is always free.
  • A normal unlock costs the price the person chose. The stores list much higher prices because each further unlock on the same day can cost double, up to 10 times that price, for people who turn that on.
  • Past their monthly limit, HabitToll asks before every paid unlock. With “Stop at my limit” on, nothing can be bought until the 1st.
  • The “9 years” in the sign-up is each person's own estimate from their answers, not a result of using HabitToll.
  • Which apps someone blocks, and how long they use them, never leave the phone. Friends see what someone pays each day and their streak, never which apps.
  • HabitToll is for adults. It isn't a parental control app.
