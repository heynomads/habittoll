HabitToll vs Screen Time on iPhone

Screen Time is built into every iPhone and is free. On iOS 27 it gives categories like Social Media a daily Time Allowance and sets hours with a Screen Time Schedule; on iOS 26 the same tools are App Limits and Downtime. HabitToll uses Apple’s Screen Time system underneath, but instead of a countdown it asks “worth it?” each time, and going in costs 10 minutes at a price you set.

The main difference

Screen Time counts minutes. Apps in a category work until their allowance runs out, and a schedule decides when they can be used at all. Without a passcode you can change all of it in a few taps, and Apple notes that on iOS 26 a limit can be ignored once it’s reached unless you block it. With a passcode someone else keeps, Screen Time becomes a hard stop.

HabitToll asks each time you open an app you picked, outside any free minutes or free hours you gave it. Walking away is one tap, free, and counted; going in costs a price you chose, so a quick scroll becomes a small purchase you decide on. The price can double with each unlock on the same day, if you turn that on, and a monthly limit makes HabitToll ask before every paid unlock past it.

When Screen Time is the better fit

  • You want a hard daily cap, and someone else can keep the passcode.
  • You want detailed reports across iPhone, iPad and Mac.
  • You don’t want to pay anything, ever.

When HabitToll is the better fit

  • You keep giving yourself more time, but a hard block feels too strict.
  • You want each open to be a decision, not a countdown.
  • You want a lock that works on your own: HabitToll’s settings lock can make easier changes wait 24 hours, or need a partner’s code if you’d like one.
  • You also use Android.

Using both

They work together. HabitToll uses Apple’s Screen Time framework to block the apps you pick, and you can keep Screen Time’s reports and schedule alongside it.

Questions

Does HabitToll need Screen Time to be on?

HabitToll asks for Screen Time access during setup; that’s how iOS lets an app block other apps. Your own Screen Time settings and reports stay as they are.

Can Screen Time charge for unlocks?

No. Screen Time has no payments. Putting a price on 10 minutes is what HabitToll adds.
