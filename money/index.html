Where your money goes.

Your unlocks pay for HabitToll. Here is where the money goes.

From every unlock

Taxes and the store's share come off first: VAT goes to the state, and Apple or Google keep their share for the store and the payment.

The rest goes to Comads OÜ, the small company in Tallinn, Estonia, that makes HabitToll. It pays for building the app, its servers and support, and ads that bring HabitToll to more people.

You decide what it costs

You pick the price of 10 minutes and a monthly limit. Walking away is free, two emergency unlocks a month are free, and the best days cost nothing.

Our pledge

Half of HabitToll's profit funds tree planting with Tree-Nation.

Profit is what's left after all HabitToll's costs, including the ads that bring it to more people, so while we grow and make up earlier losses, it may be small or zero. We pay at least once a year and publish the numbers and the receipt here after every payment.

Why not with every unlock?

Because an unlock should cost you something. If part of every unlock went to trees, paying could start to feel like doing good, and HabitToll would stop helping you use your phone less. So an unlock buys you 10 minutes, not a donation, and we fund the trees from our profit instead.

How we count

Profit is HabitToll's own result for the calendar year: what Apple and Google pay us for HabitToll, minus everything HabitToll costs, including building it, servers, support, ads, and its share of our company's shared costs (split by revenue). Before income tax.

A year with a loss means nothing to pay for that year, and earlier losses are made up first: we pay half of what's left after that.

We pay at least once a year: half of a year's profit by the end of the following June, once our accounts are done, and earlier or more often when we can. After every payment we publish here what HabitToll earned and spent, the amount, and Tree-Nation's receipt.

Trees count as funded until Tree-Nation confirms they are planted.

If we ever change this pledge, it changes only for years that haven't started, and we say so here first.
